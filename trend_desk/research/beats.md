# Research Desk: what's different about judging studies

The Research Judge reads this after Trend Desk's `beats.md` (who Meagan is and what each
destination wants). Edit it freely; standing rules from her go in `trend_desk/rules.md` (shared)
or below under "Her rules for research".

## What a study is good for

- **pitch**: an editor would pay for it. A real finding with tension, a clock or a stake, about
  people her outlets write for, published online in the last 30 days and not covered yet. A single
  observational study can carry a pitch only when the finding is striking and the caveats don't
  hollow it out. Policy, money, disparities and "the advice you were given is wrong" travel best.
- **tfm**: the study tests a claim people already repeat ("bilingual brains age better",
  "seed oils are toxic"). Best when it contradicts or complicates the claim. The study is the
  receipt, not the whole post. One post a week, so only the strongest.
- **roundup**: useful, quick and true, with its caveat in a clause. A new number a reader can use.
- **brief**: evergreen evidence for one of her sites (see the site list). Good for solid studies
  that are a weak pitch: one cohort, already covered, or a topic people search for steadily.
  Give a seed keyword people would type.

## How to judge

- **Read the caveats and the fact-check note before the headline.** The digest's headline is
  written to sell. If the caveats undo it, it's a drop or a brief, never a pitch.
- **Effect sizes keep their type.** An odds ratio is not "times more likely". A relative risk
  needs its absolute number before it headlines.
- **Association is not causation.** Say "linked to", never "causes", for observational work.
- **Animal and lab studies** are never pitches or roundup items (code enforces this).
- **Several studies, one story.** If two or more candidates point the same way, make one idea
  with all their PMIDs. That's stronger than either alone.
- **Already pitched.** If a listed topic already covers the story, drop it as "duplicate".
- **New Scientist is owned elsewhere.** Never suggest New Scientist as an outlet.
- **Audience.** Say whether it suits general consumers, older adults, or both. Most digests
  write for seniors; Meagan pitches both.
- **A bar, not a quota.** Most studies are drops, but there is no cap on keepers: route every
  study that clears the bar, however many that is on a given day. Methods papers, protocol papers,
  validation of a clinical tool, and anything only a specialist could use are drops.

## Marketing and career studies (ids starting with W)

These come from two OpenAlex digests (marketing research, career psychology), not PubMed. They
are business and workplace science, so the health framing above doesn't apply; the bar does.

- **pitch**: the main home. Marketing studies suit "marketing and AI" and consumer-money angles
  (trade or consumer outlets); career studies suit work, burnout, AI at work and pay. Set
  `audience` to `professionals` or `trade` for a trade-press pitch, `general` for consumer.
- **Say what wasn't measured.** Most are scenario experiments or surveys of intention: clicks,
  not sales; stated choice, not purchases; one company's data. That goes in `is_it_real`, and a
  study whose headline needs the unmeasured thing is a drop or a roundup line.
- **Industry partners.** A study run with a company whose interest matches the result can still
  pitch, but the conflict goes in `is_it_real`.
- **tfm** only when the study tests a claim people repeat about work or money ("open offices make
  people collaborate", "four-day weeks raise output"). **brief** rarely fits; no site covers this.
- **roundup**: a relatable, quick finding about work or shopping with its caveat in a clause.
- Drop anything only an academic or a marketer's trade association would care about.
